# Per-Clip Metrics for the Rendered Comparison Clips

Companion to `results_comparison.md`. That file reports metrics **averaged over all 1072 test
clips**; this one reports the **individual clips** that were rendered to video, one per motion
category, so the numbers can be read alongside the footage.

Generated by `scripts/per_clip_metrics.py`, which imports
`scripts/evaluate_phc_metrics_quest.py`'s own constants and formulas (`FOOT_JOINTS`, `HEAD_IDX`,
`FPS`, `GROUND_Z`, `compute_foot_skate`, `compute_gpen`) rather than re-deriving them, so these
values are directly comparable to the aggregate table — the only difference is the averaging set.

Models: `finetuned_backbone@440k` (`student_0440160.pth`, 31.7% aggregate),
`stageR_t2@442.1k` (`student_0442080.pth`, 32.6% — reward-PPO fine-tuning on top of the
fine-tuned backbone), `option2_scratch@400k` (`student_0399840.pth`, 29.4%),
`SimXR-no-vision@e1500` (upstream SimXR repo, headset-only ablation).
Videos in `output/renderings/`.

> An earlier Stage R trial (`quest_distill_finetuned_backbone_stager`, 38.3% aggregate) is
> deliberately **excluded** from this file: it was trained with the dropout/eval-mode bug that
> contaminated its PPO ratios (see footnote 12 in `results_comparison.md`), so its numbers are not
> a valid measurement of what Stage R does. `stageR_t2` is the bug-fixed rerun and is the only
> Stage R result reported here — despite having the *lower* headline success rate, which is itself
> a reason to distrust the buggy run's higher one.

## Extra columns not in the aggregate table

`eg_mpjpe_mm` decomposes into two parts that behave very differently for these models:

- **`root_drift_mm`** — how far the whole body is from where it should be (pelvis-to-pelvis).
- **`pose_err_mm`** — root-relative joint error, i.e. how wrong the *limb configuration* is once
  you subtract the body's displacement.

Motion categories follow the derived banding in `results_comparison.md` (root horizontal travel
speed of the ground-truth motion): planted <20 cm/s, semi-mobile 20-50, locomotive >50.

**The fine-tuned backbone wins every *accuracy* metric** — position, root drift, pose and
rotation — against the un-fine-tuned variant and SimXR. This clip was deliberately selected by

foot-skate 2.66 → 1.98, velocity error 17.4 → 15.9) while giving back accuracy (rotation

net loss.

Note SimXR's jitter here (291 vs our 69) — its RL policy is visibly twitchier on stationary
motion, which is consistent with it being rewarded for tracking rather than for smoothness.

**The only clip in the entire test set** where a full-body/transition motion is survived by every
model tested (dancing 0/20, workout 0/10, stretching 0/11 all fail outright for our models; yoga
has exactly one success). The fine-tuned backbone is **worst** here on global error — its root
drift (251 mm) is 8x SimXR's (30 mm) — while still holding the best root-relative pose error.

This is the split that recurs throughout: **our models pose the limbs competently but cannot keep
the body in the right place; SimXR anchors the body tightly but poses less accurately.** SimXR's
reward includes head tracking, which directly supervises global position; our action-regression
loss supervises joint targets and never directly penalises drift.

| rot_error (°) | 12.01 | 30.15 | 13.19 | 24.13 |
| vel_error (cm/s) | **29.05** | 31.48 | 30.80 | 31.22 |
| jitter (m/s³) | 711.82 | **250.66** | 887.86 | 304.36 |
| foot_skate (cm/s) | 18.64 | **7.70** | 14.17 | 13.63 |
| gpen_mean (mm) | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 | 0.00 |

**⚠️ Do not read the low `pose_err` / `rot_error` for our models as good performance.** Both of
ours *fail* this clip — this is one of 144 walking clips, on all of which the fine-tuned backbone
scores 0/144. When the policy stops tracking and stands roughly still, its limb configuration
sits near the mean pose, and root-relative metrics reward that: the reference's swinging legs
average out, so "do nothing" scores better on `pose_err` than SimXR's genuine but imperfect
walking. The metrics that expose what actually happened are `root_drift` (102 mm — the reference
walks away and our character does not follow), `jitter` (712 and 888, vs SimXR's 304 — falling and
flailing), and above all the survival flag.

This is a concrete demonstration of why aggregate `MPJPE`/`MPJRE` are weak differentiators for
this pipeline, as long noted in `results_comparison.md`: **failed rollouts can score deceptively
well on pose-shape metrics.** Success rate and root drift are the honest signals.

## What Stage R actually changed

`stageR_t2` sits on top of `finetuned_backbone@440k`, so comparing those two columns isolates
what reward-based PPO fine-tuning did. Three consistent effects across all three clips:

**It buys smoothness and physical plausibility, and pays for it in pose accuracy.** Jitter and
foot-skate improve almost everywhere — gestures jitter 68.9 → 54.8, walking jitter 711.8 → 250.7
(a 2.8x reduction), walking foot-skate 18.6 → 7.7. But `rot_error` gets consistently *worse* on
the clips the base model already handled (gestures 16.8° → 27.2°, walking 12.0° → 30.2°). This is
the same rotation-for-survival trade-off documented aggregate-level in `results_comparison.md`
(footnote 12), now visible clip by clip: the reward rewards staying upright and on-target, not
matching per-joint orientation.

**It helps most where the base model was worst.** On the yoga clip — the base fine-tuned
backbone's weakest showing (257.8 mm, 250.6 mm root drift) — Stage R improves *every* positional
metric: global error 257.8 → 190.3 mm, root drift 250.6 → 212.9, pose error 108.2 → 88.5, rotation
32.1° → 26.9°. On the gestures clip, where the base model already swept every accuracy metric,
Stage R makes it slightly worse. So Stage R is a stabiliser, not a general accuracy improvement.

**It does not fix locomotion.** All three of our variants still fail the walking clip. Stage R
reduces the flailing (jitter 712 → 251, foot-skate 18.6 → 7.7) but the character still does not
follow the reference — root drift stays ~100 mm and the episode still terminates. Consistent with
the aggregate finding that locomotion is 0/144 regardless of variant.

**Why the excluded buggy trial is not simply the better result.** Before it was dropped from this
file, the buggy trial was measured on these same three clips and lost to `stageR_t2` on pose
error, rotation, jitter and foot-skate on *every* one of them — despite carrying the higher
headline success rate (38.3% vs 32.6%). That is weak but real evidence that its aggregate
advantage came from a noisier policy surviving more often rather than tracking better, which is
consistent with the standing caveat in `results_comparison.md` that its peak is UNVERIFIED and may
be a noisy-trajectory artifact. Reported here only as the justification for excluding it.

## Pre-sim (backbone-only) metrics for the same three clips

Everything above is **post-sim** — measured on the physically simulated character. This section
is **pre-sim**: the kinematic pose the AvatarPoser backbone predicts (`q_base`) *before* physics
runs. Valid to compute open-loop because the 54-D HMD input is always derived from the
ground-truth reference motion, never from the simulator state, so a standalone forward pass
reproduces exactly what `q_base` was during the closed-loop rollout. Generated by
`scripts/per_clip_presim_gpen.py`.

**SimXR is absent by construction, not by omission.** It maps sensors directly to actions with no
intermediate pose — "no intermediate representations" is the paper's central design claim — so
there is no pre-sim pose to measure.

| Model | Category | gpen @ Z=0 | rate | gpen @ per-seq | rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| finetuned_backbone@440k | upper-body / planted | 0.00 | 0.000 | 0.00 | 0.000 |
| finetuned_backbone@440k | full-body / transition | 0.00 | 0.000 | 34.90 | 0.076 |
| finetuned_backbone@440k | lower-body / locomotion | 0.00 | 0.000 | 10.05 | 0.114 |
| option2_scratch@400k | upper-body / planted | 0.00 | 0.000 | 0.00 | 0.000 |
| option2_scratch@400k | full-body / transition | 0.00 | 0.000 | 31.47 | 0.076 |
| option2_scratch@400k | lower-body / locomotion | 0.00 | 0.000 | 8.65 | 0.088 |

Two ground conventions are reported because pre- and post-sim gpen are otherwise not comparable:
`Z=0` is the real Isaac Gym floor (matches the post-sim tables above); `per-seq` is each clip's own
GT minimum foot-joint height (matches `avatarposer_quest2_phase_a.json`). See footnote 4 in
`results_comparison.md`.

### The important result here is NOT the gpen — it's `q_base err`

**Pre-sim pose error is essentially uniform across all three categories (31–42 mm), including
walking.** The backbone reconstructs walking poses just as accurately as planted ones — 38.5 mm on
the walking clip vs 41.7 mm on the planted one. Yet post-sim that same clip is a total failure
(113 mm, terminated), while the planted clip succeeds at 82.9 mm.

So **the locomotion failure is not a kinematics problem.** The backbone knows what walking looks
like; the breakdown is entirely downstream, in converting that pose into physical control that
keeps a simulated body balanced through stepping and weight transfer. That localises the problem
to `action_head` / the distillation objective rather than to the pose estimator, and is consistent
with the frozen-backbone probe (swapping backbones changed little) and the pre-sim finding that
both backbones decode to statistically identical accuracy.

Pre-sim gpen itself is uninformative: **0.00 at `Z=0` for every clip and both models**. Head-anchored
kinematic reconstruction simply never places a joint below the floor, so the metric has no
discriminative power before physics. Only the per-sequence convention registers anything, and there
it mostly reflects how far the foot-twist joint sits above that clip's own reference minimum.

## Cross-clip summary

| Category | Best model | Margin |
|---|---|---|
| Upper-body / planted | **finetuned_backbone** | wins every accuracy metric; Stage R wins smoothness only |
| Full-body / transition | **SimXR** | best global error (8x lower root drift); stageR_t2 best pose/rotation |
| Lower-body / locomotion | **SimXR** | only model that survives at all; Stage R reduces flailing but still fails |

The picture is consistent with the per-activity analysis in `results_comparison.md`: our pipeline
is competitive-to-better than SimXR when the feet stay planted, and collapses completely once the
motion requires stepping or weight transfer.

## Caveats

1. **`survived` comes from the 128-env batched eval, not the single-env render.** The videos are
   1-env rollouts, and physics diverges between the two configurations. Renders match the eval on
   clips the policy handles comfortably (whiteboard 426/430, cooking 409/412, gestures 423/426)
   but diverge on marginal ones — the yoga renders in particular fall at 233/409 (fine-tuned) and
   151/409 (Option2) despite both being eval successes. **Treat this table as the quantitative
   claim and the videos as illustration.**
2. Single clips, not distributions. Clip 1 was selected *because* the fine-tuned backbone wins on
   it; clips 2 and 3 were forced choices (the only qualifying full-body clip, and a representative
   walking clip). None are random samples.
3. SimXR's numbers are recomputed here with **our** metric code from its own position dumps, so
   they are consistent with ours — but the paper reports over **11 selected joints** while this
   uses all 25 (see `results_comparison.md`'s metric-definition caveat), so these are not
   comparable to figures quoted from the SimXR paper's tables.
4. `gpen_mean` is 0.00 everywhere and carries no information here — expected post-fix, see
   footnote 4 in `results_comparison.md`.
